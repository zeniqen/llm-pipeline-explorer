Educational Demo Fallback Rule

When a word is not in the vocabulary, this simplified demo splits it into characters. The first character is kept as-is, and every subsequent character is prefixed with ##. All resulting characters receive ID 0 ([UNK]). Real LLMs use trained subword algorithms like BPE or WordPiece.

Educational Foundations: Vectors & Embeddings

Key theoretical principles governing representations in modern language models.

1. What is a Vector?

In machine learning, a vector is simply an ordered list of numbers. In a conceptual model, each number (or dimension) corresponds to a specific property, feature, or parameter of the input item.

2. High-Dimensional Reality vs. 3D

Real language models produce embeddings with hundreds or thousands of dimensions (such as 768, 1536, or 4096 dimensions). They cannot be plotted directly. This 3D view is an illustrative demonstration showing how geometric proximity represents conceptual similarity.

3. Static vs. Contextual Similarity

Spelling similarity and semantic meaning are completely different concepts. "The cat sat on the..." and "The bat sat on the..." share nearly all characters, but refer to different creatures. Conversely, "The cat sat on the mat" and "Kitten rests on the rug" share almost zero words, yet mean the same thing.

4. Polysemy & Context

Words often change meaning based on their surrounding context. For example, the word "light" can mean "not heavy" (a light bag) or "illumination" (a bright light). Real contextual embeddings adjust their representations dynamically based on surrounding tokens.
